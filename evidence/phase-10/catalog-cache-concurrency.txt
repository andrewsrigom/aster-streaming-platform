Workload
The baseline invoked the full owner source directly for 24 concurrent callers.
The cache path then invoked 24 callers for one cold key, preserving one compact
PostgreSQL fence read per caller while process coalescing reduced full source
loads to one. A warm call reused the exact positive entry. Two independent cache
reader instances then contended on the same Redis lease and caused one full source
load. Lease expiry/recovery and owner-token compare-delete completed the failure
boundary.

The focused 242-test Catalog suite also synchronizes concurrent requests for
`[hot,A]` and `[hot,B]`: the hot title appears in one source batch while the new
`B` title is refreshed separately, proving per-title overlap without splitting a
request's newly owned titles into one query each.

Interpretation
Bounded in-process sharing removes same-process cold-key source amplification;
the short Redis lease also coordinates the tested two-instance overlap. Every
caller still checks Catalog's current fence, so amplification reduction does not
weaken rights or publication freshness.

Limitations
- Wall times are shared-host laboratory samples. The direct baseline exercises
  the full source only, while the cache path deliberately adds fence, Redis and
  coalescing work; their elapsed values are not a throughput comparison or SLO.
- The experiment proves this finite 24-caller/two-instance workload, not arbitrary
  cluster size. Admission remains capped by the application and GraphQL limits.
