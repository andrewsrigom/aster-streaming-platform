Workload
The baseline invoked the full owner source directly for 24 concurrent callers.
The cache path then invoked 24 callers for one cold key. Exact negative-key
process coalescing reduced the owner-fence reads to one, and positive-fence
coalescing reduced full source loads to one. A warm call reused the exact
positive entry. Two independent cache reader instances contended on the same
positive lease and caused one full source load; two instances checking a cold
absent ID used one fence read and the resulting short marker. Lease
expiry/recovery and owner-token compare-delete completed the failure boundary.

The focused 245-test Catalog suite also synchronizes concurrent requests for
`[hot,A]` and `[hot,B]`: the hot title appears in one source batch while the new
`B` title is refreshed separately, proving per-title overlap without splitting a
request's newly owned titles into one query each. A second synchronized case puts
two calls on opposite sides of a visibility expiry and proves they execute two
owner fence reads, returning the title only to the eligible scope.

Interpretation
Bounded in-process sharing removes same-process cold-key source amplification;
the short Redis lease also coordinates the tested two-instance overlap. Every
shared result derives from the same current Catalog fence response, while later
requests still perform a new owner check; amplification reduction does not make
Redis the visibility authority.

Limitations
- Wall times are shared-host laboratory samples. The direct baseline exercises
  the full source only, while the cache path deliberately adds fence, Redis and
  coalescing work; their elapsed values are not a throughput comparison or SLO.
- The experiment proves this finite 24-caller/two-instance workload, not arbitrary
  cluster size. Admission remains capped by the application and GraphQL limits.
