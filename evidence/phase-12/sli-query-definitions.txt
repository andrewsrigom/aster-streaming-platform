## Synthetic workload

Six one-minute counter samples model:

- successful, failed and deliberately rejected known Router operations;
- arbitrary operation names collapsed to `other`;
- Catalog completions above and below 300 ms;
- Playback completed/unavailable plus excluded not-playable/rejected outcomes;
- Progress completed/unavailable/not-playable plus excluded stale/conflict/
  rejected outcomes;
- a second workload containing only excluded traffic.

Expected and observed recording-rule ratios at five minutes:

| SLI | Population rate | Good rate | Ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| supergraph | 4.0/s | 2.0/s | 0.5 |
| catalog title read | 2.0/s | 0.5/s | 0.25 |
| playback start | 2.0/s | 0.5/s | 0.25 |
| progress write | 3.0/s | 1.0/s | 0.3333333333333333 |

Excluded-only traffic produces no ratio series. This proves that expected
rejections and no traffic do not become availability success.

## Interpretation

The four P12-R05 definitions are executable and agree with their finite metric
sources. Router metrics retain only a known-operation bucket and one of three
outcomes. Product queries consume the released finite owner-operation/outcome
contract. Query arithmetic, exclusions and no-population behavior pass with the
exact pinned engines.

## Limitations

The workload is synthetic and proves semantics, not latency, capacity or
historical availability. The local store retains one hour, browser remote
sampling remains zero, and no 28/30-day dataset exists. Protected CI must still
prove the baked rules, private Router scrape labels and one live recorded ratio
in the packaged Docker profile before this candidate can publish.
